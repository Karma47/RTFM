### 2.1.1 Data Classification
Identifying and classifying information and assets is a critical first step in developing an
effective security program. This process involves categorising data and assets based on their sensitivity, criticality, strategic, and monetary value to the organisation.
- **Prioritise security measures:** Ensures systems and data are protected through proactive risk mitigation and safeguards.
- **Facilitates compliance:** Helps meet regulatory and industry standards efficiently.
- **Streamlines access controls:** Simplifies management of user permissions and secure access.
- **Improves incident response:** Enables faster detection, containment, and resolution of threats.
- **Reduces risk of data loss:** Protects critical information from breaches, leaks, or accidental loss.


> [!Example] Incident Response
> An incident response team can securely manage sensitive data and continuously monitor for events, triggering alerts when potential data exposure is detected. 

Common types of sensitive data:
- **PII / SPII** – Personally Identifiable Information / Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information
- **PHI** – Protected Health Information
- **PFI** – Personal Financial Information

##### Anonymisation
The process of permanently removing or altering personal data so that an individual can no longer be identified, directly or indirectly.

> [!Example] Anonymisation
> Before: `Alice Lee, alice@email.com, 91234567`
> After: `User, Age 28, Singapore`  
> 👉 No way to trace back to Alice

##### Pseudonymisation
The process of replacing identifiable information with artificial identifiers (pseudonyms), where the original identity can still be recovered using additional data.


> [!Example] Pseudonymisation
> Before: `Alice Lee, NRIC S1234567A`
> After: `User ID: U1029`
> Mapping table: `U1029 → Alice Lee`  
> 👉 Identity is hidden but **recoverable**


|Aspect|Anonymisation|Pseudonymisation|
|---|---|---|
|Reversible?|❌ No|✅ Yes (with key)|
|Identifiable?|❌ Not at all|⚠️ Indirectly|
|Security level|🔒 Higher|🔐 Moderate|
|Use case|Data sharing, analytics|Internal systems, tracking users|
|GDPR classification|Not personal data|Still personal data|

#### Data Classification
- **Gathering requirements:** Identify what data exists, its sensitivity, and regulatory or business needs.
- **Classification scheme:** Define clear categories (e.g., public, internal, confidential) to organize data by sensitivity.
- **Data labelling:** Apply tags or labels to data so its classification is visible and enforceable.
- **User training:** Educate users on how to handle, classify, and protect data appropriately.
- **Maintenance:** Regularly review and update classifications to reflect changes in data and risk.

#### Data classification in government and public organisations:

Government classification are based on the possible damage ti national security, while non-government classification focus on a broader range of harm including financial, reputation damage.

#### 2.1.2 Asset classification

Asset classification involves categorizing assets based on their importance to the organization and the sensitivity of the data they store, process, or transmit. Important aspects of asset classification to remember:

- Classify assets based on their value, criticality, and sensitivity
- Consider the data stored, processed, or transmitted by the assets
- Align asset classification with data classification
- Use asset classification to prioritize security controls and risk management efforts
